::interviewer
A music streaming app. US price $11/month. In India, the goal is growth with a path to profitability.

::candidate 00:20
Plan: (1) why you can't just convert the US price, (2) inputs for an India price, (3) pricing and packaging tactics, (4) risks like arbitrage, (5) metrics.

**1. Why not just convert $11 to rupees (~₹900)?** Because **purchasing power and willingness to pay** are very different. Average incomes in India are a fraction of US levels, and there are strong free alternatives (YouTube, ad-supported apps, local services). At ₹900/month, almost nobody would subscribe.

**2. Inputs:**
- **Purchasing power parity** and local income levels.
- **Local competitors' prices:** music streaming in India is priced at roughly ₹100–₹150 per month for individual plans, often with cheaper student and mobile-only plans.
- **Payment behaviour:** UPI dominates digital payments; many people prefer small, frequent payments; prepaid mobile culture.
- **Device usage:** mostly mobile, often a single device.
- **Cost to serve:** music licensing costs are often negotiated per market, so local deals allow lower prices.

::candidate 01:00
**3. Tactics:**
- **Price point around the local market level** (~₹119/month for individuals), with **student** and **family** plans.
- **Mobile-only plan** at a lower price (Netflix did this successfully in India).
- **Small-ticket "sachet" plans:** daily or weekly passes (e.g. ₹29/week), matching prepaid habits.
- **Strong free tier with ads** for acquisition; upgrade prompts for offline downloads and ad-free listening.
- **Telecom bundles:** partner with mobile carriers (e.g. include premium with data plans), a major distribution channel in India.
- **UPI autopay** for easy recurring payments.

::interviewer
What about people in the US using a VPN to get Indian prices?

::candidate 01:40
**Price arbitrage** is a real risk. Mitigations: require a **local payment method** (Indian cards or UPI) and billing address, check account country on sign-up, and periodically verify location. Some leakage happens, but requiring local payment methods stops most of it. The benefit of growing in a huge market outweighs the small leakage.

**4. Other risks:** low ARPU (revenue per user much lower than the US, so the cost structure must be local); churn on short plans (but they also reduce the barrier to try).

**5. Metrics:** paid subscribers and growth rate, free → paid conversion, ARPU, churn by plan type (weekly vs monthly), share of payments via UPI autopay, telecom bundle conversions, gross margin per subscriber in India.

**Summary:** set India prices based on local purchasing power and competitors (around ₹100–150 per month), add mobile-only, student, family and weekly plans, use UPI and telecom bundles for distribution, protect against arbitrage with local payment requirements, and track ARPU, conversion and margins.
