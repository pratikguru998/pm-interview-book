::interviewer
B2B SaaS for mid-market companies. Average discount ~30% off list price, on most deals. Margins and renewal pricing are suffering.

::candidate 00:25
Plan: (1) diagnose why discounting happens, (2) fixes in pricing/packaging, (3) fixes in sales process and incentives, (4) value selling tools, (5) metrics.

**1. Diagnose.** Heavy discounting usually signals one or more of:
- **List price too high** for some segments (the market doesn't value it at list).
- **Weak value story:** reps can't articulate ROI, so they compete on price.
- **Incentives:** reps are paid on bookings (total contract value), not on price or margin, so discounting to close faster is rational for them.
- **End-of-quarter pressure:** discounts concentrated in the last weeks of a quarter.
- **Competitive pressure** in specific deals.

I'd analyse deal data: discount level by segment, deal size, rep, competitor present, timing in the quarter, and correlation between discount and win rate. Also listen to sales calls and talk to reps.

::interviewer
Assume the data shows smaller companies get the biggest discounts, and discounts spike at quarter end.

::candidate 01:10
That suggests two separate problems: **(a) packaging doesn't fit smaller companies** (the list price is too high for their needs), and **(b) incentive/timing issues** (quarter-end pressure).

**2. Pricing/packaging fixes:**
- Create a **lower-priced "Starter" tier** with fewer features for smaller companies, so they get an honest lower price without discounting the full product. This protects the list price of the main tier.
- Clear **volume-based price breaks** published in the price book, so reps don't improvise.

**3. Sales process and incentives:**
- **Discount approval levels:** e.g. up to 10% the rep decides, 10–20% manager approval, above 20% VP/deal desk approval.
- **Give-get rules:** a discount only in exchange for something: multi-year contract, upfront payment, a case study or reference, faster signature.
- **Commission tied to net price** (or margin), not just bookings.
- Smooth quarter-end pressure: forecasting discipline, and avoid compensation structures that reward last-minute deals at any price.

**4. Value selling:**
- **ROI calculator** and customer case studies with quantified results.
- Training reps on discovery: understand the customer's pain and quantify the cost of the problem.
- Competitive battle cards.

**5. Metrics:** average discount % (overall and by segment), discount frequency, win rate (make sure it doesn't collapse), average contract value, gross margin, renewal price uplift, and quarter-end discount concentration.

**Summary:** diagnose with deal data. Here, smaller customers and quarter-end deals drive discounting. Fix the packaging with a Starter tier, add discount approval and give-get rules, tie commissions to net price, and equip reps to sell on value, tracking discount levels alongside win rate and margin.
