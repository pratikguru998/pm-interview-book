::candidate 00:25
My approach: (1) understand the economics and the case for raising, (2) estimate the impact (price elasticity and break-even churn), (3) segment, (4) mitigation, (5) test and decide, (6) metrics.

**1. Case for raising:**
- Spotify's margins are thin because music licensing takes a large share of revenue, so price is one of the few levers.
- **Perceived value has grown:** podcasts, audiobooks (hours included), better personalization.
- **Competitors** (Apple Music, YouTube Music, Amazon Music) have raised prices, so the relative position stays similar.

::candidate 00:55
**2. Impact: break-even churn.** If we raise from $11 to $12 (+9%), each remaining subscriber pays more. We break even if we lose fewer than:

**Break-even churn = price increase ÷ new price = $1 ÷ $12 ≈ 8.3%** of subscribers.

So if fewer than ~8% of subscribers cancel *because of* the increase (beyond normal churn), revenue increases. Historically, music streaming has shown relatively low price sensitivity: people listen daily, and playlists and libraries create switching costs. So incremental churn is likely much lower than 8%. But the risk differs by segment.

::interviewer
How would you estimate the churn effect?

::candidate 01:25
- **Past price increases:** analyse churn after previous increases in other markets.
- **Willingness-to-pay surveys** and conjoint analysis.
- **Market tests:** raise prices in a few comparable markets first, or test on new sign-ups.
- **Engagement-based prediction:** heavy listeners churn less; model churn probability by engagement segment.

**3. Segment:**
- **Heavy daily listeners:** low sensitivity.
- **Light users:** higher sensitivity; may downgrade to Free.
- **Students:** very price-sensitive; keep the student plan unchanged.
- **Family/Duo plans:** may handle the increase better per person.

**4. Mitigation:**
- Communicate **added value** (audiobooks, new features) with the increase.
- **Grandfather** existing subscribers for 1–2 months.
- Keep **lower-priced options** for sensitive users (e.g. a basic tier without audiobooks, or ad-supported Free).
- Offer **annual plans** at a discount to lock in loyalty.

::candidate 02:15
**5. Decision:** I'd recommend raising the individual plan by **~$1** in the US, keep student pricing unchanged, offer a basic tier for price-sensitive users, and emphasize new value, if the market test shows incremental churn well below the break-even (~8%).

**6. Metrics to watch (3–6 months):** incremental churn vs control or historical baseline, downgrades to Free/basic, net revenue and ARPU, new subscriber conversion rate, customer sentiment, and competitor reactions.

**Summary:** compute the break-even churn (~8% for a $1 increase), estimate actual churn by segment using past increases, tests and engagement data, protect sensitive segments (students, light users) with cheaper options, and roll out with clear value communication, monitoring churn and net revenue.
