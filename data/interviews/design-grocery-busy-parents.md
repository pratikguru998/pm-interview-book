::interviewer
Amazon Fresh, online. Goal: increase retention and order frequency among families.

::candidate 00:30
**Goal:** make Amazon Fresh the effortless default for feeding a family, increasing weekly ordering households.

A key reframe: busy parents don't want to "shop for groceries". **The job is "feed my family well with minimal mental load and time."** Shopping is just one step. Plan: persona → full job journey → pains → prioritize → solutions → recommendation → metrics.

::coach
Reframing the job from "shop" to "feed the family" opens up much better solutions. This is the most valuable move in the whole answer.

::candidate 01:00
**Segments of parents:** dual-income parents with young kids, single parents, parents of teenagers (volume, picky eaters), parents with dietary restrictions in the family. I'll pick **dual-income parents with kids under 12**: very time-poor, high grocery spending, and a very repetitive weekly routine.

**Persona:** Dana, 38, two kids (5 and 9), works full time. Every Sunday she spends about an hour planning meals and ordering, and still runs out of milk on Wednesday.

::candidate 01:30
**Journey:**
1. **Decide what to cook this week** → hardest step: decision fatigue, kids' preferences, budget, healthy.
2. **Make a list** → checks the fridge, forgets things.
3. **Order** → searches item by item (40+ items).
4. **Delivery** → finding a slot, substitutions.
5. **Cook** → on weeknights, 30 minutes max.
6. **Mid-week gaps** → runs out of staples, extra trip to the store.

**Pains:** meal planning fatigue (biggest), list-building, repetitive ordering, running out of staples, waste of unused fresh food.

**Prioritize:** the **planning + list-building** pain is the root. It's weekly, mentally draining, and online grocers barely address it, while delivery is already commoditized.

::candidate 02:10
**Solutions:**
1. **Family meal planner:** set household info (ages, dislikes, allergies, budget, cooking time). Each week get 5 suggested dinners; swap with one tap. **Builds the cart automatically**, minus pantry items you likely still have.
2. **Staples autopilot:** learns consumption rhythms (milk every 4 days) → suggests or auto-adds to a recurring order, with an easy confirmation.
3. **"Rescue dinner" express:** a 30-minute delivery meal kit for chaotic nights.
4. **Kids' picks:** kids choose one meal per week from a picture menu. They're more likely to eat it, and it's a fun ritual.
5. **Leftover-aware planning:** plans meals that share ingredients to reduce waste.

::interviewer
Meal planning apps exist and most people abandon them. Why would this be different?

::candidate 02:45
Standalone planners fail because they **stop at a recipe list**: you still have to make the list and shop, so the effort saved is small and the habit breaks. Ours **ends in a filled cart and a delivery**, so the plan *is* the shopping. Plus it learns from real purchases, which makes suggestions better over time. And it's embedded where Dana already orders weekly, so no new habit is needed.

::candidate 03:15
**Evaluate:** the meal planner tackles the root pain and increases basket size (fresh ingredients), but quality of suggestions is critical. Staples autopilot drives frequency and retention with low effort. Rescue dinner monetizes moments of need. Kids' picks is a delighter.

**Recommendation:**
- **V1:** family meal planner → one-tap cart + staples autopilot.
- **V2:** kids' picks, rescue dinners, leftover-aware planning.

**Metrics:**
- **North Star:** households placing an order 4+ weeks out of 5 (habit).
- % of carts created from the planner; planner weekly retention; staples autopilot adoption; time to checkout.
- **Business:** orders per household per month, basket size, 90-day retention vs non-planner households.
- **Guardrails:** substitution and out-of-stock rate for planned meals, waste complaints, unwanted auto-adds (cancellations).

**Summary:** Busy parents' real job is feeding the family with minimal mental load. A meal planner that turns into a filled cart, plus staples on autopilot, removes the hardest weekly decisions and the mid-week gaps, driving weekly ordering habits.
