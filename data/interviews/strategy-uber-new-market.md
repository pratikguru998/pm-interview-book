**Objective:** choose cities where Uber can reach **marketplace liquidity** (short wait times, high fulfilment) quickly and reach **positive contribution margin** within a reasonable time, say 18–24 months.

Approach: (1) define criteria, (2) weight and score candidates, (3) validate with qualitative checks, (4) pilot playbook and milestones.

::candidate 00:45
**1. Criteria:**

| Category | Indicators |
|---|---|
| **Demand** | Urban density, population, car ownership (lower = better), tourism, nightlife, airports, public transport gaps, smartphone penetration |
| **Supply** | Available drivers (underemployment, gig work culture), vehicle availability and cost, fuel costs |
| **Regulation** | Legal status of ridesharing, licensing requirements, taxi union strength, data rules |
| **Competition** | Local players and their strength, incumbents' network density |
| **Economics** | Income levels and willingness to pay, expected fares, take rate, payment methods (cards vs cash) |
| **Strategic value** | Gateway to a region, synergy with Uber Eats, brand presence |

::candidate 01:30
**2. Weighting and scoring.** For profitable growth, I'd weight: demand 25%, supply 20%, regulation 20%, competition 15%, economics 15%, strategic 5%. Score candidate cities 1–5 on each, then rank.

Example (illustrative):

| City | Demand | Supply | Regulation | Competition | Economics | Weighted score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| City A (dense, tourist hub) | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | **3.9** |
| City B (large, strong local rival) | 5 | 4 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 3.6 |
| City C (mid-size, wealthy) | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **4.0** |

::interviewer
City B has the biggest demand. Why would it rank lower?

::candidate 02:10
Because a strong local rival with network density means Uber would face expensive subsidy wars for both riders and drivers, which hurts the profitability objective. Marketplaces have strong local network effects: the incumbent with more drivers has shorter waits, which attracts more riders. Breaking that is expensive. If the strategic value were very high (e.g. a key regional gateway), I'd reconsider, or consider partnership/acquisition instead of a head-on launch.

::candidate 02:40
**3. Qualitative checks:** local regulatory consultations, driver supply interviews, payment ecosystem, safety environment, cultural fit (women's safety concerns, cash usage).

**4. Launch playbook & milestones:**
- **Supply first:** sign up drivers with incentives before launch; airport and nightlife zones first.
- **Concentrated launch:** start in the dense core, then expand the service area.
- **Milestones:** median ETA under 5 minutes in the core within 3 months; fulfilment rate above 90%; driver utilization rising; contribution margin trending to breakeven by month 18.
- **Kill / pause criteria:** regulatory blocks or no progress toward liquidity after 6 months.

**Summary:** Choose cities by weighting demand, supply, regulation, competition and economics, with a bias towards cities where Uber can reach liquidity without costly subsidy wars. Validate qualitatively, launch with a supply-first, dense-core playbook, and track liquidity and margin milestones with clear stop criteria.
