**1. Ecosystem:** viewers (attention), creators (supply), advertisers (monetization). Shorts only succeeds if all three grow together.

**2. Metrics:**

| Side | Metrics |
|---|---|
| **Viewers** | Daily active Shorts viewers; Shorts watch time per viewer; % of users under 25 using Shorts weekly; retention of Shorts viewers |
| **Creators** | Daily uploads; % of creators uploading weekly; new creators starting on Shorts; creator retention; creator earnings from Shorts |
| **Advertisers** | Shorts ad revenue; RPM (revenue per 1,000 views); advertiser adoption of Shorts formats |
| **Ecosystem** | Shorts → long-form crossover: subscribers gained via Shorts, long-form views from Shorts viewers |

**3. North Star for Shorts:** **daily active Shorts viewers who watch at least N Shorts** (an engaged-viewer threshold). Attention is the strategic battleground against TikTok, and engaged viewers drive creator incentives and ad revenue.

**Supporting:** share of daily Shorts viewers aged 18–24 (the strategic segment) and creator upload volume (supply health).

::interviewer
What if Shorts grows but people watch less long-form YouTube?

::candidate 01:30
That's the main risk: **cannibalization**. Long-form monetizes better per minute (more ad slots, higher RPM), and long-form creators are the core of YouTube's library. I'd measure:
- **Long-form watch time per user** among Shorts viewers vs a holdout group with limited Shorts exposure.
- **Total YouTube watch time and ad revenue per user**, not just Shorts.
- **Long-form creator earnings.**

Whether some cannibalization is acceptable depends on the alternative: if users would otherwise spend that time on TikTok, YouTube is better off capturing it with Shorts, even at lower RPM. The holdout comparison tells us whether Shorts is **net positive** for YouTube's total time and revenue.

::coach
Cannibalization questions are best answered with a holdout and a "what's the counterfactual?" framing: would the time have gone to a competitor anyway?

::candidate 02:10
**4. Guardrails:** long-form watch time, long-form creator earnings, total ad revenue per user, user satisfaction surveys, harmful content prevalence in Shorts.

**5. Overall success:** Shorts is successful if (a) it grows engaged short-form viewing, especially among younger users, (b) it brings new creators into the ecosystem, (c) Shorts monetization improves over time, and (d) holdouts show YouTube's *total* time and revenue per user are higher with Shorts than without.

**Summary:** Evaluate Shorts across viewers, creators and advertisers, with engaged daily Shorts viewers as the North Star, and judge it overall by whether it increases YouTube's total watch time and revenue against a holdout, not just by Shorts' own growth.
