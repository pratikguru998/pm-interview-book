Estimate the annual smartwatch market in India, in units.

::interviewer
Smartwatches only, all price points.

::candidate 00:15
**Approach:** estimate the installed base (people who own a smartwatch), then divide by the replacement cycle to get annual unit sales, adjusting for first-time buyers.

**Formula:** annual units ≈ smartwatch owners ÷ replacement cycle (years) + growth from new buyers.

::candidate 00:30
**1. Target population:**
- India population: ~1.43B.
- Age 15–55 (most likely buyers): ~60% → ~860M.
- Smartphone users in that group: a smartwatch needs a phone to pair with. ~70% → **~600M**.

**2. Ownership rate:** smartwatches became very affordable in India (some under ₹2,000, ~$25) thanks to local brands (Noise, boAt, Fire-Boltt). Urban young people adopted them quickly. Segment:
- Urban smartphone users (~40% of the 600M = 240M): ~30% own one → 72M.
- Rural/semi-urban (~60% = 360M): ~10% → 36M.
- Total owners: **~108M → ~110M**.

**3. Replacement cycle:** cheap watches break or get replaced quickly: **~2–2.5 years**. Use **2.5 years**.

**4. Annual units:** 110M ÷ 2.5 ≈ **44M replacements**, plus new first-time buyers (market still growing): say +10M → **~50M units/year**.

::interviewer
Industry trackers said shipments were around 50+ million at the peak, then declined. Why might the market decline?

::candidate 01:35
Market saturation among urban young buyers, consumers delaying replacements (watches lasting longer, little new functionality), and quality concerns with very cheap models leading some users to stop wearing them. So the market can shrink even with a large installed base, mainly because replacement cycles lengthen and first-time buyers slow down.

**Sensitivity:** ownership rate and replacement cycle drive most of the answer. If the cycle is 3 years, units fall to ~37M + new buyers.

**Summary:** ~600M likely buyers × ~18% ownership ≈ 110M owners, replaced every ~2.5 years, plus new buyers ≈ **~50M units per year** at peak.
