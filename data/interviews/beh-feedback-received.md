::interviewer 00:00
Tell me about a time you received difficult feedback.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** About a year into my first PM role, my manager shared feedback from my engineering team in a 1:1: *"Engineers feel you hand them solutions instead of problems. They feel like order-takers."*

**Task.** It stung, because I thought I was being helpful by coming prepared with detailed specs and mockups. But I knew that if the team felt like order-takers, I'd lose their best ideas and their ownership.

::interviewer
How did you react?

::candidate 00:35
**Action.**
1. **I listened and asked for specifics** instead of defending myself. My manager gave two examples: specs where I'd defined the exact UI and technical approach before discussing it with the team.
2. **I asked the team directly.** In a retro, I said: "I've heard I bring solutions too early. I want to change that. What would work better for you?" The tech lead suggested involving them when the problem is defined, before any designs exist.
3. **I changed my process:**
   - Specs now start with the **problem, evidence, goals and constraints**, with no solution section in the first draft.
   - I run a **solution workshop** with engineering and design for each significant feature.
   - Engineers join selected **user interviews**, so they understand the problem first-hand.
4. **I checked in** a month later with the team and my manager to see if they noticed a difference.

::interviewer
What changed as a result?

::candidate 01:20
**Result.**
- In the next workshop, an engineer proposed using an existing service in a new way, which **cut a project's scope in half**: an idea I wouldn't have come up with.
- Team engagement in planning increased noticeably (more ideas, more pushback in a healthy way).
- In my next 360 review, "collaboration" was rated my top strength by the engineers.

**Learning.** Feedback that stings is often the most valuable. My job isn't to have all the answers; it's to make sure the team deeply understands the problem, so the best solution can come from anyone.
