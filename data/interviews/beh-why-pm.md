::interviewer 00:00
Why do you want to be a product manager?

::candidate 00:05
**Past: how I discovered it.** I started my career as a software engineer at a logistics startup. I kept finding myself pulled into conversations about *what* we should build and *why*, not just how. At one point, our delivery-tracking feature had poor adoption, so I asked if I could join customer calls. I talked to about 15 dispatchers and learned they didn't need more tracking detail. They needed **alerts when a delivery was at risk of being late**. I proposed that change, we built it, and **weekly active usage of the feature went up about 3×**. That was the moment I realized the problem I enjoy most is figuring out the *right* thing to build.

**Present: what energizes me.** I love the combination the PM role requires: deeply understanding users, using data to make decisions, working closely with engineering and design, and connecting it all to business outcomes. I also enjoy the communication side: aligning people around a clear goal. Over the past year, I've been doing more of this in my current role: writing specs, running user interviews, and prioritizing our team's backlog with my manager.

**Future: why this role.** Your company is solving [specific problem, e.g. "making small business payments simple"], and I've seen that pain first-hand [e.g. "my family runs a small shop and struggles with this"]. My engineering background will help me work closely with your platform teams, and I'm excited to own outcomes rather than just outputs, like improving merchant activation, which I saw is a current focus for your team.

::interviewer
What do you think is the hardest part of being a PM?

::candidate 01:25
Prioritizing and saying no while keeping trust. There are always more good ideas than capacity, and every "no" disappoints someone. Doing it well means being clear about goals, transparent about trade-offs, and consistent, so people understand the reasoning even if they disagree. I've practised this when managing our team's backlog, but I know it gets harder at larger scale.
