::candidate 00:25
I'll define success as **new grads getting interviews**. Applications alone can be gamed (more applying ≠ better outcomes), and hires are hard to attribute and slow. Interviews are a strong, measurable signal of matching quality. For LinkedIn, better matches mean recruiters get more value from paid products (Recruiter, job posts).

Plan: user understanding → journey → pains (both sides) → prioritize → ideas → metrics.

::candidate 00:55
**New grad segments:** students from top universities with strong pipelines (well served by campus recruiting), **students from less-recruited schools** (no pipeline), career switchers from bootcamps, international students (visa constraints). I'll focus on **students from less-recruited schools**. They rely most on online job search and struggle the most.

**Persona:** Aisha, final-year business student at a state university. Small network, internship at a local company. Applied to 120 jobs on LinkedIn, got 4 replies.

::candidate 01:30
**Journey:** search "entry level marketing" → hundreds of results, many asking for 3+ years of experience → unsure if she qualifies → applies with the same résumé → silence → loses motivation.

**Seeker pains:**
1. "Entry level" jobs that aren't entry level.
2. Can't tell if she's a good fit.
3. Applications disappear with no response.
4. No network or referrals, while referred candidates have much higher interview rates.

**Recruiter pains:** hundreds of unqualified applications per entry-level post; hard to identify potential without experience.

**Prioritize:** fit clarity (2+1) and **referrals (4)**. Referrals are a huge lever and LinkedIn's unique asset is the graph, including **alumni networks**.

::candidate 02:10
**Ideas:**
1. **"True entry-level" filter + fit score:** NLP parses requirements; the score compares with the grad's skills, coursework, projects; shows "You match 7 of 9 requirements; missing: SQL."
2. **Alumni referral bridge:** on each job, show alumni at that company. One-click "ask for advice/referral" with a guided, respectful message template; alumni get a "help a fellow graduate" prompt.
3. **Application status:** nudge recruiters to send one-click status updates ("Not moving forward") with templates.
4. **Skills assessments:** short verified tests that add a badge, giving signal without experience.

**Prioritize:**

| Idea | Interview impact | Unique to LinkedIn | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit score + filter | Medium–high (better targeting) | Medium | Medium |
| Alumni referral bridge | **High** | **High (graph)** | Medium |
| Status updates | Low (morale) | Low | Low, needs recruiter adoption |
| Assessments | Medium | Medium | High |

**Recommendation:** alumni referral bridge + fit score.

::interviewer
Won't alumni get spammed?

::candidate 03:10
Yes, that's the main risk. Mitigations: rate limits (e.g. 5 requests per week per grad), only show alumni who opt in to "open to helping graduates", guided templates that ask for advice before a referral, and quality signals (fit score above a threshold before a referral request is allowed). Plus reward alumni with recognition badges. Measure alumni response rate and opt-out rate.

::candidate 03:40
**Metrics:**
- **North Star:** interview rate per application for new grads (via status updates and surveys).
- Referral requests sent → accepted; % of applications above a fit threshold; time to first interview.
- **Recruiter side:** % of qualified applicants on entry-level posts, recruiter satisfaction.
- **Guardrails:** alumni opt-out and report rate, application volume dropping too much.

**Summary:** Grads from less-recruited schools struggle with fit and lack of networks. A fit score plus an alumni referral bridge uses LinkedIn's graph to turn applications into interviews, with safeguards against spamming alumni.
