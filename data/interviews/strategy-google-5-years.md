::candidate 00:40
**1. Trends:**
- **Generative AI answers:** users increasingly expect direct, synthesized answers rather than links (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's own AI Overviews).
- **Agents:** AI that *does things*: books, buys, fills forms.
- **Multimodal input:** camera (Lens), voice, screenshots.
- **Shifting behaviour among younger users:** discovery via TikTok, YouTube, Reddit for "real people" opinions.
- **Ecosystem pressure:** publishers worry about losing traffic; regulators scrutinize defaults and self-preferencing.
- **Personal context:** users may opt in to give AI context from email, calendar, documents.

**2. Durable user jobs:** find a fact fast; understand a topic; make a decision (compare, buy); get something done (book, plan); find local places; stay informed.

::candidate 01:30
**3. Vision:** *Search evolves from "ten blue links" to a trusted assistant that understands what you're trying to accomplish and helps you finish it, while sending people to the best sources.*

Example: "Plan a 4-day family trip to Lisbon in April under $3,000" → itinerary with options → compare flights and hotels → book with confirmation steps → reminders. Every claim cites sources; you can dig in.

**4. Pillars:**

| Pillar | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| AI answers with citations | Synthesized answers, transparent sources, links that drive traffic | Health or finance answers with source quality labels |
| Agentic tasks | Complete multi-step tasks with partners | Book a restaurant, return an order |
| Multimodal | Search with camera, voice, video | "What's wrong with my plant?" |
| Personal (opt-in) | Uses your context, privately | "When is my flight and what's traffic like?" |
| Human perspectives | Surface forums, reviews, creators | "What do people actually think of this laptop?" |

::interviewer
This sounds like it kills Google's ads business. Fewer clicks, fewer ads.

::candidate 02:30
That's the central tension. I'd address it in three ways:
1. **Commercial queries stay valuable.** In decision and task flows, there are natural places for sponsored options, clearly labelled ("Sponsored hotel"), and ads may convert better because intent is clearer.
2. **New models:** transaction or affiliate fees for completed tasks (bookings), and premium subscriptions for advanced AI capabilities (Google already sells AI subscriptions).
3. **Protect the ecosystem:** if publishers lose traffic, content quality drops, and AI answers get worse. So design answers that **cite and link generously**, and consider revenue sharing for content used.

The deeper point: if Google doesn't make this shift, competitors will, and the ads business erodes anyway. **Disrupt yourself rather than be disrupted**, while carefully managing the transition with experiments.

::coach
Business model tension is THE follow-up in vision questions. A strong answer acknowledges it, offers mitigations, and explains why the change is necessary anyway.

::candidate 03:20
**6. Risks:** answer accuracy (hallucinations in health/finance), regulatory action (self-preferencing in agent flows), publisher backlash, privacy with personal context, compute cost per query.

**Metrics:**
- **User value:** task completion rate, time to answer, satisfaction; share of queries where users don't need to reformulate.
- **Trust:** accuracy and groundedness of AI answers (evals), citation click-through.
- **Ecosystem:** traffic sent to publishers, creator/publisher health.
- **Business:** revenue per query (ads + transactions + subscriptions), compute cost per query.
- **Competitive:** query share among under-25 users.

**Summary:** In 5 years, Google Search should be a trusted assistant that helps people get things done, with cited AI answers, agent-completed tasks, multimodal input and opt-in personal context. The ads model evolves toward high-intent sponsored options, transactions and subscriptions, while protecting publishers so the information ecosystem stays healthy.
