::interviewer
New app, consumers first with plans for teams later. Costs are mostly storage and sync, which are cheap per user.

::candidate 00:20
I'll define both models, then evaluate them against the criteria that matter for this app.

- **Freemium:** a free plan forever with limits; users pay for premium features.
- **Free trial:** full product for a limited time (e.g. 14 days), then pay or lose access.

**Criteria:**
1. **Time to value:** how long until users experience the core value?
2. **Network/viral effects:** do free users bring other users?
3. **Cost of free users.**
4. **Market size and competition.**
5. **Switching costs / lock-in over time.**

::candidate 00:50
**Evaluation for a note-taking app:**

| Criterion | Note-taking app | Favours |
|---|---|---|
| Time to value | Value **accumulates over weeks/months** (notes pile up, search, organization) | Freemium: a 14-day trial ends before users are hooked |
| Viral effects | Sharing notes, collaborative pages bring others in | Freemium |
| Cost of free users | Low (storage, sync) | Freemium is affordable |
| Market | Crowded (Notion, Evernote, Apple Notes, Google Keep), many free options | Freemium: a trial makes it hard to compete with free alternatives |
| Lock-in | The more notes, the higher the switching cost | Freemium: build lock-in, then monetize |

**Recommendation: freemium**, with premium features that matter more as usage grows:
- **Free:** unlimited notes, basic sync across 2 devices, basic search.
- **Premium (~$5–8/month):** unlimited devices, offline access, larger uploads, version history, advanced search (inside PDFs/images), AI features (summaries, Q&A over notes).
- **Teams (later):** shared workspaces, admin controls, per seat.

::interviewer
Freemium usually converts only a few percent. Isn't that a problem?

::candidate 01:40
Freemium conversion is typically low (often 2–5%), but it works when (1) the free base is large and cheap to serve, and (2) free users create value: word of mouth, shared notes, and a **pipeline for team plans** (people bring the tool to work). To improve conversion, I'd design **natural upgrade moments**: hitting the device limit, needing offline access while travelling, searching inside a PDF. And I'd add a **reverse trial**: new users get Premium free for 14 days, then drop to Free. That shows them premium value early while keeping freemium's long-term benefits.

**Metrics:** free → paid conversion (by cohort over 6–12 months), activation (notes created in week 1), retention, upgrade triggers hit, cost per free user, viral coefficient (invites/shares), team plan pipeline.

**Summary:** for a note-taking app, value builds over time, sharing drives growth, free users are cheap, and competitors are free, so freemium (with a reverse trial and upgrade moments tied to growing usage) beats a short free trial.
