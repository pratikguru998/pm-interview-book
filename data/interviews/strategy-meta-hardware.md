Why does Meta invest so heavily in VR and AR hardware?

::candidate 00:05
Let me structure it: (1) Meta's strategic vulnerability, (2) what owning a platform gives you, (3) why VR/AR specifically, (4) risks, (5) my assessment and what I'd do as a leader.

::candidate 00:20
**1. The vulnerability: platform dependency.** Meta's apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp) run on operating systems owned by Apple and Google. That means:
- **Rules can change overnight.** Apple's App Tracking Transparency (2021) limited ad tracking on iOS, and Meta said it would cost it billions in revenue.
- **App store fees and policies** apply to Meta's in-app purchases.
- **Data access and distribution** depend on competitors.

Meta's leadership has openly said that being at the mercy of other platforms shaped their thinking.

**2. What owning a platform gives you:**
- Control of distribution and app store economics (fees from developers).
- Direct access to user data and identity (within privacy rules).
- Ability to shape the experience end to end.
- A long-term position in "the next computing platform", the way mobile replaced PCs.

::candidate 01:15
**3. Why VR/AR?** Meta bets that the next major computing platform will be **spatial and wearable**: glasses that overlay information on the world, and immersive headsets. Its core business is **social connection**, and "presence" (feeling like you're with someone) is a natural fit. Recent focus has also shifted toward **AI glasses** (e.g. Ray-Ban Meta) as a lighter, more practical step: camera, audio, an AI assistant, no heavy headset.

::interviewer
They've lost tens of billions on Reality Labs. Is it worth it?

::candidate 01:45
It's a huge bet. The reasoning: Meta generates very large profits from ads, so it can fund a long-term option that, if successful, could be worth far more and reduce existential platform risk. It's similar to how Google funds moonshots, or how Amazon invested in AWS for years. But the risk is real:
- Adoption of VR headsets has been slower than hoped (comfort, price, limited killer apps).
- Apple and Google could win the AR glasses platform anyway.
- Investors may lose patience.

**4. Risks summary:** adoption risk, competition (Apple Vision Pro, Google/Samsung XR), hardware execution, privacy concerns (cameras on faces), opportunity cost.

::candidate 02:30
**5. Assessment and what I'd do:**
- The *strategic logic* (escape platform dependency, own the next platform) is sound.
- I'd **rebalance toward AI glasses**: lightweight, socially acceptable, useful today (photos, music, calls, AI assistant), and a stepping stone to full AR.
- Set **milestones**, not just vision: monthly active devices, retention after 3 months of ownership, developer revenue, cost per unit trending down.
- Keep VR focused on proven use cases (gaming, fitness, social) rather than broad "metaverse" promises.

**Summary:** Meta invests in VR/AR to escape dependence on Apple and Google and to own the next computing platform, where its social mission fits naturally. The logic is sound, but execution and adoption risk are huge, so I'd emphasize practical AI glasses and hold the effort to clear adoption milestones.
