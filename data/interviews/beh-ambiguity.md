Illustrative story: use your own. Ambiguity stories should show how you **created structure**: breaking the unknown into questions, gathering evidence quickly, testing, and converging on a clear plan.

::interviewer 00:00
Tell me about a time you worked through a highly ambiguous situation.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** At a marketing software company, the CEO asked me to "figure out our small business strategy". We had grown with mid-size companies, but small businesses (SMBs) were signing up and leaving quickly. There was no defined problem, team, budget or metric, just a feeling that we were "missing out on SMBs".

**Task.** Turn that vague mandate into a concrete, fundable plan within six weeks.

::interviewer
Where did you start?

::candidate 00:35
**Action.**
1. **I broke the ambiguity into questions:** Who exactly are "SMBs" for us? What do they need? Where do we lose them? Is this segment worth serving?
2. **Data first (week 1):** I worked with our analyst to compare funnels. SMB **trial-to-paid conversion was half** the mid-market rate, and SMBs dropped off most during setup (connecting their email list and building a first campaign).
3. **Talk to users (weeks 1–3):** I interviewed **25 SMB owners**, including those who churned. A clear theme emerged: "Setup is too complex; I don't have a marketing team; I just want to send a good email this week."
4. **Size the opportunity:** I estimated the SMB market and revenue potential, showing it was worth pursuing, but only with a simpler product experience, not a separate product.
5. **Hypotheses and quick tests (weeks 3–5):** I proposed three hypotheses and ran two cheap tests:
   - A **guided setup wizard** with templates for common SMB campaigns.
   - A **simplified pricing page** explaining a starter plan.

::interviewer
What were the results?

::candidate 01:35
**Result.**
- The setup wizard test increased **SMB trial-to-paid conversion by 22%**.
- The pricing page test had no significant effect, so we dropped it. That was useful learning too.
- I presented a strategy doc: target segment (SMBs with 1–10 employees), the core problem (setup complexity), the plan (a dedicated SMB onboarding squad), and OKRs (SMB conversion from 8% to 12%).
- Leadership funded a squad of 5, and SMB conversion reached **11.5%** within two quarters.

**Learning.** In ambiguous situations, I structure the unknowns into questions, reduce uncertainty with fast evidence (data, interviews, cheap experiments), and share progress early so leadership can steer. A clear problem statement is often the most valuable output.
