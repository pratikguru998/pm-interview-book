Illustrative story: use your own. "Saying no" stories should show courage, data and an alternative, and that the relationship survived or got stronger.

::interviewer 00:00
Tell me about a time you had to say no to an important stakeholder.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** I was PM for the analytics product at a B2B SaaS company. Our VP of Sales asked my team to build a custom reporting feature for a prospect worth about **$200k in annual revenue**. They wanted it within 6 weeks to close the deal.

**Task.** My team was halfway through a retention project, a usage-health dashboard and in-product guidance, projected to save over **$1M a year** in churn. Building the custom report would have delayed that by about two months.

::interviewer
How did you handle it?

::candidate 00:35
**Action.**
1. **I took the request seriously.** I asked to join a call with the prospect to understand what they actually needed. It turned out their main need was getting our data into **their own BI tool** (Tableau) to combine it with other data, not necessarily a native report in our product.
2. **I evaluated it against our priorities** with numbers: the custom report served one prospect; the retention project affected hundreds of existing customers.
3. **I met the VP in person** (not over Slack), acknowledged the importance of the deal, and walked through the trade-off openly.
4. **I offered an alternative:** a **data export/API connector** to their BI tool, which my team could build in about a week, and added "native custom reports" to the roadmap backlog with a rule: if 5+ customers asked, we'd prioritize it next quarter.

::interviewer
How did the VP react?

::candidate 01:20
Initially frustrated: they had already told the prospect "yes". But when they saw the data and the alternative, they agreed to pitch the BI connector. I also offered to join the next call with the prospect to explain the solution, which helped sales feel supported rather than blocked.

**Result.**
- The prospect accepted the BI connector and **signed the $200k deal**.
- The retention project shipped on time and reduced churn by about **15%** over the next two quarters.
- The connector turned out to be useful for other customers too (~30 adopted it).
- The VP started involving me earlier in large deals, before commitments were made to customers.

**Learning.** A "no" lands well when it comes with transparent reasoning, an understanding of the real need, and an alternative. Joining the customer call was key: the stated request (custom report) wasn't the real need (data in their BI tool).

- **Data-based trade-off** communicated respectfully and in person.
- **Alternative** that won the deal *and* protected priorities.
