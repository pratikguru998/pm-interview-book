Should a news app monetize through ads or subscriptions?

::candidate 00:05
Clarifying questions:
1. What kind of news app: a publisher's own app (like The New York Times) or an aggregator (like Google News)?
2. Audience size and loyalty?
3. Brand positioning: premium journalism or mass-market?

::interviewer
A publisher's app with quality journalism. A few million monthly readers, a loyal core.

::candidate 00:20
Plan: compare the models on several criteria, then recommend (likely a hybrid), with metrics.

**Ads:**
- ✅ Free access → large reach; monetizes casual readers.
- ❌ Requires very large scale (revenue per user per month is small); ad prices fluctuate; ad-tech dominated by big platforms.
- ❌ **Incentive problem:** optimizes for clicks and pageviews, which pushes toward clickbait and can hurt journalism quality and trust.
- ❌ Ad blockers and privacy changes reduce revenue.

**Subscriptions:**
- ✅ Predictable recurring revenue.
- ✅ **Aligned incentive:** rewards quality and loyalty (readers pay because content is valuable).
- ✅ Reader data relationships (first-party) and lower dependence on platforms.
- ❌ Limits reach; many people are unwilling to pay for news; competition from free news.

::candidate 01:00
| Criterion | Ads | Subscriptions |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per user | Low | High (from payers) |
| Reach | High | Lower |
| Quality incentive | ❌ Clickbait risk | ✅ |
| Predictability | ❌ | ✅ |
| Fit with "quality journalism" brand | Weak | Strong |

**Recommendation: subscription-first hybrid (metered paywall).**
- Readers get a few **free articles per month** (with ads), then are asked to subscribe. This keeps discovery and SEO while converting loyal readers.
- **Registration wall** first (free account) to build a relationship and collect first-party data, then the paywall.
- **Bundles:** news + games + cooking + audio (NYT has shown this increases value and retention).
- **Lighter ads** for non-subscribers; ad-free or premium experience for subscribers.
- **Annual plans and introductory offers** to reduce churn.

::interviewer
How would you decide the number of free articles?

::candidate 01:45
Test it. The trade-off: more free articles → more ad revenue and reach, but fewer people hit the paywall. Run experiments with different meter limits (e.g. 3, 5, 10 free articles a month) by segment, measuring **subscription conversion, total revenue (subs + ads), and engagement**. Some publishers use **dynamic paywalls**, adjusting based on each reader's likelihood to subscribe: loyal readers hit the paywall sooner, casual ones get more free articles.

**Metrics:** subscriber growth, conversion rate from registered users, ARPU, churn (especially after intro offers end), engagement of subscribers (articles read per week, a leading indicator of retention), ad revenue from non-subscribers.

**Summary:** for a quality publisher, subscriptions align revenue with journalism quality and loyalty, so a metered paywall hybrid (free articles with ads, registration, then subscription, with bundles) balances reach and revenue, with the meter tuned by experiments.
