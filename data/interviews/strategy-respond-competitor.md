::interviewer 00:00
A competitor launches a free version of your paid product. What do you do?

::candidate 00:05
That's a high-stakes situation, and the worst thing is to panic. Let me ask some questions to make it concrete:
1. What's our product and business model?
2. Who's the competitor: a startup, or a giant that can afford to be free (e.g. bundling)?
3. Has there been any impact yet on sign-ups or churn?

::interviewer
You're a B2B project management SaaS, $10 per user per month, mostly small and mid-size companies. The competitor is a big tech company that just added a free project tool to its office suite. Nothing measurable yet; it launched two weeks ago.

::candidate 00:35
OK, a classic **bundling threat**, like Microsoft Teams vs Slack. The competitor doesn't need to make money from this tool; it's protecting its suite.

My approach: (1) understand the competitor's goal and offering, (2) assess our exposure by segment, (3) assess our moats, (4) consider options, (5) recommend and set triggers for further action.

::candidate 01:00
**1. Competitor's motivation and product.** A big suite vendor bundling a free tool aims to increase suite stickiness and block standalone tools. Their tool is probably "good enough" for basic task lists, integrated with their email and docs, but less deep than ours. Their distribution is massive: already installed in many of our customers' companies. Their weakness: broad focus, slower innovation on niche features, less flexibility for workflows.

**2. Exposure by segment:**

| Segment | Switching risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Very small teams (1–10 users), basic usage | **High** | Price sensitive, simple needs, already on the suite |
| Mid-size teams with complex workflows | Medium | Value integrations, automations, reporting |
| Teams using us across departments with heavy customization | Low | High switching costs |
| Companies on a different office suite | Low | Not affected by the bundle |

**3. Our moats:** depth of features (automations, portfolios, reporting), integrations with 100+ tools, data and workflow lock-in, brand/community, speed of innovation.

::interviewer
What data would you look at first?

::candidate 01:55
Leading indicators, segmented:
- Trial sign-ups and trial→paid conversion since the launch (top of funnel).
- Churn and downgrade requests, especially with reasons mentioning the competitor.
- Win/loss notes from sales.
- Usage depth by customer: who uses only basic features (high risk).
- Overlap: which of our customers use that competitor's suite (via email domains/integrations).

That gives me a **risk map** within weeks.

::candidate 02:20
**4. Options:**
1. **Match: launch a free tier** for small teams (e.g. up to 5 users, basic features). Protects the funnel, but cannibalizes some revenue.
2. **Differentiate on value:** double down on depth (automations, AI, reporting), positioning as the tool for serious teams.
3. **Integrate with the competitor's suite:** become the best project tool *inside* their ecosystem (calendar, email, docs integrations), so the bundle isn't a reason to leave.
4. **Retain key accounts:** proactive outreach, annual contract incentives, migration help.
5. **Price change:** lower price — risky, signals weakness, and damages revenue.

**5. Recommendation (phased):**
- **Now (weeks):** build the risk map; proactive success outreach to high-risk accounts; offer annual plans with a discount; publish a comparison guide focused on our depth.
- **Next (1–2 quarters):** **launch a limited free tier** for small teams to protect the top of the funnel (so new small teams don't default to the bundle), **deepen integrations** with the competitor's suite, and invest in differentiating features (automations, AI).
- **Don't:** cut prices across the board.

**Triggers to escalate:** if mid-size churn rises above a threshold (e.g. +2 points) or trial conversion drops >15%, consider repackaging (e.g. cheaper starter tier).

**Metrics:** gross revenue churn by segment, net revenue retention, trial→paid, win rate vs the competitor, free-tier → paid conversion.

**Summary:** Don't panic or race to the bottom. Understand who's actually at risk, protect the funnel with a limited free tier, integrate with their suite rather than fight it, and double down on depth for teams who need more than "good enough", with clear triggers for further action.
