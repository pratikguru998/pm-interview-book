Illustrative story: use your own. Amazon-style "customer obsession" stories are strongest when you go deep to understand the root cause for one customer, then fix it **for many customers**.

::interviewer 00:00
Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** I was PM for data exports at an analytics SaaS company. A mid-size e-commerce customer told their account manager they would cancel at renewal because "exports are too slow and painful". They were worth ~$80k a year.

**Task.** The account team asked me to speed up exports. But I wanted to understand what the customer was really trying to do.

**Action.**
1. **I asked to shadow their analyst** for half a day over video. I watched her export three large CSV files every Monday, clean them in Excel for two hours, and build **one weekly chart** for her leadership team.
2. **The real problem** wasn't export speed; it was the **manual weekly reporting workflow**. Faster exports would save 10 minutes; the real pain was 2+ hours a week.
3. **Short term (for them):** we set up a **saved dashboard** replicating her chart directly in our product, and scheduled it to email her leadership every Monday. Took me and a solutions engineer a day.
4. **Long term (for everyone):** I checked our data. Many accounts exported the same reports weekly. That became the case for a **"scheduled reports"** feature on the roadmap.

::interviewer
What happened?

::candidate 01:15
**Result.**
- The customer **renewed** and later expanded their contract by 20%; the analyst told us we gave her "two hours of her week back".
- The **scheduled reports** feature launched the next quarter; **~30% of accounts** adopted it within 6 months, and export-related support tickets dropped significantly.

**Learning.** Going above and beyond doesn't mean doing exactly what the customer asks faster. It means understanding the job they're trying to do. Watching real work revealed the true problem, and the fix scaled to many customers.
