::interviewer 00:00
Describe a time you had to influence a team you had no authority over.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** I was the PM for checkout at an e-commerce company expanding into Indonesia. Our conversion there was low, and a big reason was that we didn't support a popular local e-wallet. Payments integrations were owned by a separate **platform payments team**, and the wallet wasn't on their roadmap for the next two quarters.

**Task.** I needed the integration within one quarter to hit our Indonesia growth target, without being able to direct their team.

::interviewer
How did you approach it?

::candidate 00:35
**Action.**
1. **I learned their goals first.** I met their PM and asked about their OKRs. Their key goals were **reducing payment failure rates** and **lowering processing costs**.
2. **I found the overlap.** I researched the wallet and found it had **lower fees** than card processing in Indonesia and higher success rates (many users there don't have credit cards). So the integration would help *their* goals too, not just mine.
3. **I brought evidence.** I showed that **30% of checkout abandonment** in Indonesia happened at the payment step, and estimated the revenue impact if we added the wallet.
4. **I reduced their cost.** I offered one of my team's engineers to work with them on the integration, so it wouldn't take their full capacity.
5. **We co-presented.** Their PM and I wrote a one-page proposal together and presented it to both of our directors as a joint plan.

::interviewer
Was there any resistance?

::candidate 01:25
Yes. Their engineering lead was worried about maintaining another payment method long-term. We addressed it by agreeing on clear ownership after launch (their team owns it, with documentation and monitoring dashboards my engineer helped build), and by including a success review after 3 months: if the volume was low, we'd revisit it.

**Result.**
- The integration shipped in **8 weeks**.
- Indonesian checkout conversion increased by **12%**.
- The payments team **reduced processing costs** in that market, which counted toward their OKR.
- Our teams collaborated on two more local payment methods afterwards.

**Learning.** Influence starts with understanding what the other team cares about. If you can frame your request as progress toward *their* goals, and reduce their cost to say yes, you rarely need authority.
