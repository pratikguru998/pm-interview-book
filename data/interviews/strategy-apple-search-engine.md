::candidate 00:20
**Context:** Google reportedly pays Apple a very large sum every year (reported in court as around $20 billion in a recent year) to be Safari's default search engine. That's close to pure profit. So any decision has a massive opportunity cost.

Structure: (1) reasons to build, (2) reasons not to, (3) options in between, (4) recommendation and triggers.

::candidate 00:40
**1. Reasons to build:**
- **Strategic control:** search is a gateway to information and commerce; AI assistants are changing how people search. Apple doesn't want to depend on a rival.
- **Privacy brand:** a privacy-first search engine aligns with Apple's positioning.
- **Regulatory risk:** US antitrust cases challenge default search deals; the payments could shrink or end.
- **AI future:** Siri and Spotlight could answer many queries on-device.

**2. Reasons not to build:**
- **Lose a huge, high-margin payment.**
- **Quality gap:** a web index, ranking and freshness at Google's level takes years and massive investment. Users would notice worse results, and might switch default anyway.
- **Business model conflict:** search is funded by ads; Apple's privacy stance makes a targeted ads business awkward.
- **Not core competency** at web scale.

::interviewer
If the regulator bans the deal, what then?

::candidate 01:35
Then the calculation changes: the payment disappears, so the opportunity cost drops. Apple would likely use a **choice screen** (as in the EU) and invest more in its own answer layer. That's why I'd build **optionality now**: improve Siri and Spotlight answers, work with AI partners, and develop selective search capabilities, so Apple can move quickly if the deal ends.

::candidate 02:00
**3. Options:**
1. Full general-purpose search engine: very costly, uncertain quality.
2. **Answers layer:** Spotlight, Siri and AI handle many queries (weather, contacts, apps, factual answers, local) on-device and with partners, routing others to the default engine.
3. **Partner with AI providers** for generative answers (Apple has integrated third-party AI models into Siri).
4. Keep the status quo.

**4. Recommendation:** don't build a full Google competitor now. Pursue **option 2 + 3**: an AI-powered answers layer that reduces dependence and builds capability, while keeping the default deal as long as it's legally allowed. **Triggers:** if the deal is banned or significantly reduced, accelerate investment and use a choice screen.

**Metrics:** % of queries answered by Apple's own layer, user satisfaction, privacy perception, cost per query, readiness indicators (index coverage for key verticals).
