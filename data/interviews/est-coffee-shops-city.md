How many coffee shops can a city of 2 million people support?

::candidate 00:05
Clarifying questions:
1. What kind of city: a US city, European, Asian? Coffee culture varies.
2. Coffee shops only (cafés like Starbucks or independents), not restaurants or convenience stores that also sell coffee?

::interviewer
A typical US city. Cafés only.

::candidate 00:15
**Approach:** demand side (cups bought per day) divided by supply side (cups a shop needs to sell to survive).

**Formula:** Shops = daily café cups demanded ÷ cups per shop per day needed to be viable.

::candidate 00:30
**Demand:**
- Population: 2,000,000.
- Adults (18+): ~78% → 1.56M.
- Share of adults who buy coffee from a café on a given day: many drink coffee daily, but most make it at home or at work. Maybe **~20%** of adults buy café coffee on a given day → 312,000 buyers.
- Cups per buyer: ~**1.2** (some buy twice) → **~375,000 cups/day**.
- Add visitors and students under 18 buying drinks: +5% → **~390,000 cups/day**.

**Supply (viability):**
- A café needs revenue to cover rent, staff, ingredients. Say a small café needs ~$1,500–2,000/day to break even.
- Average ticket ~$5.50 (coffee + sometimes food). That's ~300–350 transactions/day.
- I'll use **~350 cups/day** per shop on average (big chains sell much more, small independents less).

**Calculation:** 390,000 ÷ 350 ≈ **1,100 coffee shops**.

::interviewer
Does that sound right to you?

::candidate 01:15
**Sanity check:** 2M ÷ 1,100 ≈ one café per **1,800 people**. US cities vary a lot: coffee-heavy cities like Seattle have much higher density; car-centric cities have lower. One per ~1,500–3,000 people seems plausible for an average US city.

**Sensitivity:** the % of adults buying daily matters most: 15% → ~830 shops; 25% → ~1,400.

**Range: ~800–1,400 coffee shops**, central estimate ~1,100.
