Great. I'll choose **ages 4–7, early readers**, and the goal: **encourage children to choose and read books on their own**, while being safe and easy for parents. For IKEA, that also means affordable, flat-pack and fitting small apartments.

Plan: users → needs of kid and parent → prioritize → ideas → recommendation → metrics.

::candidate 01:00
**Users:**
- **Child (the end user):** age 4–7, can't read spines, chooses by cover, short attention span, loves ownership.
- **Parent (the buyer):** wants safety, tidiness and a reading habit; has limited space and budget.

**Age segments:** toddlers (0–3: chewing, climbing, board books), **early readers (4–7)**, older kids (8–12: chapter books, larger collections). I'm picking 4–7 because that's when independent reading habits form, and today's standard shelves (spines facing out) don't work for them.

::candidate 01:40
**Journey (Mia, 5):** wants a book at bedtime → looks at the shelf → sees only spines, can't tell books apart → pulls out several, books fall → gives up or asks a parent → mess on the floor.

**Child pains:** can't recognize books by spine, can't reach the top shelves, the shelf is boring, no sense of progress.
**Parent pains:** tip-over risk, mess, books get damaged, the habit doesn't stick, small rooms.

**Priority:** (1) **discoverability by cover** + reachability → directly drives choosing to read; (2) **safety** → must-have; (3) tidiness → helps parents; (4) motivation → delighter.

::candidate 02:20
**Ideas:**
1. **Front-facing "sling" shelves** at child height, showing covers like a bookstore.
2. **Rotating "book of the week" spotlight slot:** parents rotate 6–8 books visible at a time, the rest in a bin below. Fewer choices means easier decisions.
3. **Integrated reading nook:** a bench with a cushion and a lamp built into the side.
4. **"Reading tree" wall decal:** the child adds a leaf sticker for every book finished.
5. **Easy-return bins** with picture labels (animals, bedtime, facts) so tidying becomes sorting.
6. **Safety:** wall-anchoring kit included, rounded corners, low centre of gravity.

**Evaluate:** front-facing slings and safety are core (high impact, low cost). Rotation slot + bins balance discovery and storage. The nook is a premium add-on (more cost and space). The reading tree is cheap delight.

::candidate 03:10
**Recommendation: a low (≈90 cm) wall-anchored shelf** with 3 front-facing tiers, 2 picture-labelled bins at the bottom, a reading tree decal, and an optional nook bench module. Flat-pack, under €80.

**Metrics:** unit sales and reviews mentioning "my child reads more", return rate, safety incidents (target zero tip-overs), attach rate of the nook module. Qualitative test: a 4-week home trial with 20 families measuring books picked independently per week vs a standard shelf.

::interviewer
How would you know it actually increases reading?

::candidate 03:40
Run a home study: 20 families with the new shelf, 20 with a standard shelf, matched by child age. Parents log, or a simple QR-code check-in records, "books the child picked independently" for 4 weeks. Compare the averages. It's not perfect, but it gives a directional signal before scaling.
