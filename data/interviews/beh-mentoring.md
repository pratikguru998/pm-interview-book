::interviewer 00:00
Tell me about a time you helped someone on your team grow.

::candidate 00:05
**Situation.** On my team, Priya, a talented junior product designer, produced excellent designs but struggled to present them to senior leadership. In reviews she'd get interrupted, lose her train of thought, and her ideas got overruled, even when they were well researched.

**Task.** I wasn't her manager, but as her PM partner I wanted to help her succeed, both for her growth and because her work was strong and we needed it to land.

**Action.**
1. **I asked first.** In a 1:1 I asked how she felt the reviews went. She said she found them stressful and wanted to improve. Coaching works better when someone wants it.
2. **Gave a structure:** I shared a simple presentation framework I use: start with the **user problem and evidence**, then the **recommendation**, then **alternatives considered**, then **what you need from the room**.
3. **Practice sessions:** we did 30-minute rehearsals before the next three reviews. I played a tough executive and asked the hard questions in advance.
4. **Stretch opportunity:** for our next quarterly roadmap review, I suggested she present the design direction herself, with me as backup only if needed.
5. **Feedback loop:** after each review, we spent 10 minutes on what worked and what to adjust.

::interviewer
How did it go?

::candidate 01:15
**Result.**
- In the quarterly review, Priya presented confidently, handled tough questions from our VP, and her proposed direction was approved.
- The VP praised her presentation in the follow-up email to the team.
- Six months later she was promoted, and she now coaches newer designers using the same framework.

**Learning.** Growing people means combining structure, practice, real opportunities and honest feedback, and then **stepping back** so they own the success.
