Storage-focused plans, global.

::candidate 00:15
**Approach:** freemium funnel. Everyone gets 15 GB free across Gmail, Drive and Photos; some users run out and pay.

**Formula:** Revenue = active Google accounts × % paying for storage × average annual price.

::candidate 00:30
**1. Active Google accounts:** Gmail has about **1.8B+** users; Android devices number in the billions, and most require a Google account. I'll use **~2.5 billion** active accounts.

**2. % paying:** who runs out of 15 GB?
- Heavy photo/video users (Google Photos stopped free unlimited storage in 2021, pushing more people to pay).
- People with years of email attachments.
- Families sharing plans.

Segment:
- Light users (email only), ~50%: almost never pay.
- Photo-heavy smartphone users, ~40%: maybe 7–8% pay.
- Power/work users, ~10%: maybe 10% pay.
Weighted: 0 + 0.4×0.075 + 0.1×0.1 = 0.03 + 0.01 = **~4%**.

Google has publicly said Google One passed 100 million subscribers, which is ~4% of 2.5B, consistent.

**3. Average annual price:** plans range from ~$20/year (100 GB) to ~$100/year (2 TB), plus higher tiers. Most people choose the cheapest plan. Lower prices in emerging markets too. Average ~**$25/year**.

::candidate 01:30
**Calculation:**
- Subscribers: 2.5B × 4% = **100M**
- Revenue: 100M × $25 = **$2.5B per year**

::interviewer
What would push this number higher?

::candidate 01:45
- **AI bundles:** higher-priced tiers with AI features raise ARPU significantly.
- **Photos and video growth:** more high-resolution content from better phone cameras.
- **Family plans** and upselling to bigger tiers.
- **Device bundles** (e.g. Pixel with included storage that converts to paid).

**Sensitivity:** average price is the biggest uncertainty: if the mix shifts to AI tiers, revenue could be multiple times higher.

**Summary:** ~2.5B accounts × ~4% paying × ~$25/year ≈ **$2.5B per year** from storage subscriptions.
