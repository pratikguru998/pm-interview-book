US. They partner with DoorDash and Uber Eats today. Goal: grow delivery profitably while keeping the customer relationship.

::candidate 00:30
Structure: (1) delivery economics, (2) Starbucks' situation, (3) options (build, partner, hybrid), (4) evaluation, (5) recommendation.

**1. Delivery economics:** delivery is a **density game**. A courier's cost is spread across orders; profitable delivery needs many orders per hour in a small area and decent order values. Platforms like DoorDash aggregate demand from thousands of restaurants, which a single brand can't match.

**2. Starbucks' situation:**
- **Small ticket size** (~$10–15 per order): high delivery cost relative to order value.
- **Quality degrades fast**: hot drinks cool, iced drinks melt, foam collapses.
- **Peak-heavy demand** (morning): a dedicated fleet would sit idle much of the day.
- **Strength:** a hugely successful app and Rewards programme: a direct customer relationship and data.

::candidate 01:20
**3. Options:**
1. **Build own fleet:** full control, data, and experience; very high fixed costs, low utilization, labour management.
2. **Pure partner** (status quo): fast and flexible; lose some margin (commissions) and customer data when orders come through third-party apps.
3. **Hybrid:** order inside the **Starbucks app** (keeping data, Rewards, customization), fulfilled by partner couriers ("white-label delivery", e.g. DoorDash Drive).

**4. Evaluation:**

| Criterion | Build | Partner | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | ❌ High fixed | ✅ Variable | ✅ Variable |
| Speed to scale | ❌ Slow | ✅ | ✅ |
| Customer data & loyalty | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Quality control | ✅ | Medium | Medium |
| Utilization risk | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ |

::interviewer
Doesn't Starbucks lose control of quality with partners?

::candidate 02:05
Partly, and that's the main argument for building. But quality issues are mostly about **time and packaging**, which Starbucks can control: delivery-optimized packaging (sealed lids, insulated bags), a limited delivery menu (no drinks that degrade quickly), a smaller delivery radius, and priority prep timing when the courier is close. Those fix most of the problem without the cost of a fleet.

::candidate 02:25
**5. Recommendation: don't build a fleet. Use the hybrid model.**
- Push delivery ordering through the Starbucks app with Rewards (keep data and loyalty).
- Partner couriers for fulfilment; negotiate volume-based rates.
- Invest in delivery-specific quality: packaging, menu, timing, radius.
- Revisit building only in extremely dense markets (e.g. Manhattan) if volume and utilization justify it.

**Metrics:** delivery order margin, % of delivery orders via the Starbucks app vs third-party apps, delivery rating (temperature/quality), delivery customers' visit frequency and retention.

**Summary:** Delivery economics favour aggregators, and Starbucks' small tickets and peaky demand make its own fleet unprofitable. A hybrid model keeps the customer relationship in Starbucks' app while partners handle couriers, with quality controlled through packaging, menu and timing.
